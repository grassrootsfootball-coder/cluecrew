## 3. Rulings — entries 7 to 9, genuinely new to me

### 7. `en-determiner-omitted` — APPROVE WITH THE HINT AS REWRITTEN

The drafted hint ends "Read the line aloud and listen." A child sitting an 11+ paper
cannot read aloud. It is also the only hint in the library that asks her to do
something the room forbids.

**childHint (rewritten):**

A single thing needs a, an or the in front of it. Say the line in your head and check.

Description approved as written.

### 8. `en-narrator-voice-as-fact` — APPROVE WITH THE HINT AS REWRITTEN

The drafted hint coaches the key. Its third sentence — "Or is it the writer telling
you what a room full of people decided?" — supplies the correct reading rather than
blocking the error. A child who reads it has been told the answer is the crowd.

This is the Q7 fault, and it is the same shape as the whole-text case: the entry names
a child who takes a reported opinion as fact, so any hint that names the reporting
hands her the item.

**childHint (rewritten):**

Before you take this as true, ask whose opinion it is.

Description approved as written — the boundary against `en-extreme-option-pull` is
right, and whose-voice against pull-of-absolutes is a real distinction.

### 9. `en-relative-pronoun-choice` — APPROVED AS WRITTEN, with a note

Both fields approved. The note is that the description covers four errors — which for
people, whose for who, whom in a subject slot — and the hint reaches three of them.
Nothing in "a person, a thing, or something belonging to someone" helps the child
choosing *whom* where *who* belongs, because that is a case error rather than a
reference error.

Not a blocker at one item. If this entry ever carries a *whom* distractor, it needs a
second clause or a second entry.
