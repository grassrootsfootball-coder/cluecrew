## ENG-004-AGG-07 — technique · Tier 3 · lines 29-31
*Tier: The child has to see that a comparison measures a feeling rather than describing a real event.*

**Line 30 says the task was harder than 'bearding a lion in its den'. Why does the writer compare it to that?**

- A. To show that Matthew is bold, like a lion hunter.  · `en-comparison-vehicle-misread **(PROPOSED)**`
  - Takes the daring figure the comparison implies as a description of Matthew, missing that he stands on the frightened side of it.
- B. To show that Matthew felt sad about the mistake.  · `en-emotion-label-approximation`
  - Picks a neighbouring feeling; lines 29 and 31 point to dread, not sadness.
- **C. To show how frightening an everyday task felt to shy Matthew.**  ← key
- D. To show that Matthew had once faced real danger.  · `en-figurative-taken-literally`
  - Reads the comparison as something that happened rather than a picture of a feeling.

> Look at lines 29 to 31. The writer says the task was harder than 'bearding a lion in its den'. Then he names the task: walk up to a girl. Line 31 says Matthew groaned, so the comparison is measuring his dread.

Hint: Ask what the comparison measures: the task, or the man.

## ENG-004-AGG-08 — technique · Tier 4 · lines 33-46
*Tier: Two paragraphs must be held together and read as one deliberate choice about how we see the girl.*

**The writer describes the girl twice. Lines 35 to 40 give what 'an ordinary observer' would see. Lines 41 to 46 give what 'an extraordinary observer' might see. Why does the writer do this?**

- A. To show that the girl changed while she sat waiting.  · `en-parallel-read-as-sequence **(PROPOSED)**`
  - Reads two ways of seeing as two moments in time; the passage gives no change.
- B. To show that Matthew saw both of these things.  · `en-cause-agent-swap`
  - Gives the seeing to Matthew, though line 34 says he was not looking at her.
- C. To show that nobody could ever really know her.  · `en-extreme-option-pull`
  - Stretches to nobody and ever where the passage is measured.
- **D. To show there is far more to the girl than her plain looks.**  ← key

> Look at line 34, then lines 35 and 41. Line 34 tells you Matthew was not looking at her. The writer then shows the same girl twice, to two different watchers. What changes is the watcher, not the girl.

Hint: Ask what changes between the two descriptions.
