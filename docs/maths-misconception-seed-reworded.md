## Place Value & Decimals

**03. Decimal alignment**
Line up the decimal points in a perfectly straight line before you start adding.

**08. Negative number inversion**
Picture a thermometer. Which number is colder and further down the scale?

## Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication & Division

**12. Forgetting to exchange**
If the top column does not have enough, you need to exchange from the next one.

**13. Adding the exchange twice**
Cross out your carried number once you add it. This stops you from counting it again.

## Fractions, Decimals & Percentages

**23. Additive equivalent fractions**
Whatever you multiply the top by, you need to multiply the bottom by too.

**24. Inverted fraction of an amount**
Divide by the bottom to find one part. Then multiply by the top for your total.

**30. Fractions of different wholes**
Fractions show proportions, not exact sizes. A quarter of a small pizza differs from a massive one!

## Measurement

**34. Starting measurement at 1**
Line up the edge of your object with the zero line. Do not start at one.

**39. Perimeter vs Area (Multiplying)**
To find the perimeter, imagine walking around the very edge. Add up the whole journey!

## Geometry

**43. Line length dictates angle**
An angle measures the turn between two lines. It does not measure how long they are.

**46. Reversed coordinates**
Remember the rule. Go along the corridor (x) before you go up the stairs (y).

**47. Translation by gap**
Pick one corner on the first shape. Count the squares to that same corner on the new shape.

**48. Reflection as sliding**
A mirror flips things backwards. The closest point to the mirror stays closest in the reflection.

**49. Universal symmetry**
Only isosceles and equilateral triangles have lines of symmetry. A scalene triangle has none at all!

## Statistics, Ratio & Proportion

**51. Additive scaling**
Recipes scale up by multiplication. If you double the eggs, you need to double the cakes too!

**52. Ratio to fraction confusion**
A fraction is part of the whole. First, add the ratio parts to find the total.

**54. Reading the gap**
Follow the flat top of the bar straight across to the axis to read the number.

**56. Incomplete mean**
The mean shares things equally. Once you have the total, divide by how many items there are.

**58. Restrictive mean**
The mean is an average. Your final answer can be a decimal or a brand new number.

**60. Ignoring the outside of a Venn diagram**
Every piece of data needs a home! If it does not fit in a circle, write it outside.
