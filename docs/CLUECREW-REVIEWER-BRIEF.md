# CLUECREW — SPECIALIST REVIEWER BRIEF
### Everything you need for the role · August 2026

**Welcome. You are the specialist reviewer: the person whose signature makes a question real. Nothing reaches a child on this platform without a qualified teacher approving it — the system will not publish without it. Read this once, then work from §4 onwards.**

---

## 1. WHAT CLUECREW IS

An 11+ preparation platform for children aged 8–11, GL-style formats first, currently live with its Verbal Reasoning district free to any family. Three more districts are in build: Maths, Non-Verbal Reasoning, English.

Questions are drafted by an AI pipeline and are worthless until a specialist has checked them. Our public promise is that a qualified teacher signs off every question before a child sees it. You are that sentence.

Two things we never do: reproduce content from real exam papers (every item is original, and you are also our check on that), and speak harshly to a child — "wrong", "incorrect", "fail" and anything like them never appear in text a child reads.

## 3. HOW THE WORK REACHES YOU

You will receive **review packs** — PDF or HTML documents designed for reading and annotating, on paper if you prefer. You do not have to use the platform: decisions can be recorded against your name from a written reply, with an audit trail distinguishing *whose decision it was* from *who typed it in*. If you would rather work in the platform directly, that takes fifteen minutes to set up.

Packs arrive with a decisions page at the back and space to write against each item.

## 4. THE STANDARDS (what you are checking, per item)

1. **Solve it yourself first**, without looking at the marked answer. Mismatch → return it. (Maths items carry a machine-verified solution, so keys should be right — you are judging whether the *question* deserves its key.)
2. **Devil's advocate every distractor.** If any wrong option is defensibly correct, the item is ambiguous. This is the highest-value check you make: four unanswerable items were found this way that five automated gates had missed.
3. **Distractors must be earned.** Each wrong answer is tagged to a named misconception and must be exactly what a child making *that* mistake produces. Our hints teach from these tags, so a mistagged distractor teaches the wrong lesson. If you cannot derive the wrong answer by executing its misconception, return it.
4. **Tier fit.** T1 secure Year 4 · T2 early Year 5 · T3 secure Year 5 (the centre of gravity) · T4 Year 5+/early Year 6 stretch · T5 super-selective multi-step. Name what makes an item its tier.
5. **Reading age ≤9** on stems and options — we test the skill, not decoding. Plain sentences, UK contexts, nothing assuming a particular home life or disadvantaging EAL children.
6. **Originality smell-test.** If an item feels like a real paper you have used, flag it. Automated similarity checks run, but your professional memory is a second net.
7. **Voice.** Child-facing hints and explanations are warm and specific. Flag anything cold, or anything telling a child what they got rather than what to do next.

**What good review looks like here.** We would rather you found problems than passed things. The most valuable session so far was one where a pack was rejected outright — that rejection led to a whole question type being brought under automatic checking, four unanswerable items removed, and the difficulty system rebuilt after someone noticed Tier 4 held easier words than Tier 1. Nothing is signed off by default and nothing is urgent enough to rush.

## 5. THE THREE KINDS OF REVIEW

**Item review** (VR, Maths, English) — individual questions judged against §4.

**Template review** (NVR only, much lighter) — NVR items are not written; they are generated from 13 templates. You review the *template* by examining 30 sample items per difficulty tier, then sign that template version. Every item it generates inherits your approval. If the template changes, its fingerprint changes and the signature voids automatically — enforced in code, not by memory. **What we need is a plain sentence per template naming its fingerprint: "I confirm the machine-series template at fingerprint XXXX is approved for live use."** Fingerprints are printed on each template's sign-off line.

**Library work** — authoring or approving misconceptions: the named mistakes children actually make, each with a child-facing hint. The most teacherly work in the project, and the part where a specialist's knowledge is irreplaceable. The 97-entry maths library was written this way.

## 6. HOUSE RULES FOR ANYTHING CHILD-FACING

Applies to hints and explanations you write or approve:
- **16 words maximum per sentence.** Split rather than compress.
- **Reading age ≤9.**
- **No "wrong", "incorrect", "error", "mistake", "fail", "must", "should have"** — and not just those words, the *stance*. A hint says what the child should do next, never what their answer was.
- Quoted passage text and the specific word an item is testing are exempt; the system handles that automatically.
