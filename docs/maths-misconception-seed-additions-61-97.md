## NEW MISCONCEPTION ENTRIES (61–97)

### Number & Place Value

**61. Reading the wrong place value column** — Reading a digit as if it sits in the neighbouring column (e.g., hundreds instead of tens).
*Hint:* Check the columns carefully. Ones, tens, hundreds, thousands. Which column does that digit actually sit in?

**62. Reading a digit at face value** — Giving the face value of a digit while ignoring its place value completely.
*Hint:* Look at where the digit sits. Its position changes its actual value!

**63. Misaligned addition carry** — Adding or losing a carried number into the wrong column.
*Hint:* Only carry into the very next column on the left. Don't skip a space!

**64. Leading-zero ordering slip** — Placing a zero first when building the smallest possible number.
*Hint:* A whole number cannot start with a zero. Pick the next smallest digit to lead.

**65. Incorrect rounding place** — Rounding to the nearest ten or thousand instead of the requested hundred.
*Hint:* Check the question again. Which column are you being asked to round to?

**66. Negative number miscount** — Miscounting steps when crossing zero, or ignoring the minus sign entirely.
*Hint:* Draw a quick number line. Count each step carefully as you cross past zero.

**67. Dropped internal placeholder** — Forgetting the zero placeholder for an empty column when writing a number from parts.
*Hint:* Does a column have nothing in it? Use a zero to hold that empty space!

**68. Wrong power of ten scaling** — Dividing by 10 or 1000 instead of 100 when finding how many hundreds fit into a number.
*Hint:* One hundred has two zeros. Make sure you divide by 100, not a different number!

### Calculation

**69. Dropped calculation carry** — Forgetting to add a carried ten or hundred to the next column's product or sum.
*Hint:* Write your carried numbers clearly, and always remember to add them to the next column.

**70. Two-digit column totals** — Writing the full two-digit total in a single column without carrying.
*Hint:* Only one digit can go in each column! Carry the extra over to the left.

**71. Forgetting part of a multi-step problem** — Leaving out a whole place value, a second item's cost, or stopping halfway.
*Hint:* Reread the question. Have you used all the numbers and finished every single step?

**72. Wrong operation chosen** — Adding instead of subtracting, multiplying instead of dividing, or vice versa.
*Hint:* Look at the words in the question. Are things getting bigger, smaller, or being shared?

**73. Digit-by-digit division** — Dividing each digit on its own and dropping the remainders completely.
*Hint:* Leftovers matter! If a column doesn't divide perfectly, carry the remainder to the next digit.

### Fractions, Decimals & Percentages

**74. Comparing fractions incorrectly** — Believing fractions with different bottom numbers cannot be compared, or judging by the bottom number.
*Hint:* Give both fractions the same bottom number. Then it is easy to compare them fairly.

**75. Incomplete fraction of an amount** — Finding the value of one part but forgetting to multiply for the top number.
*Hint:* You found one part perfectly! Now check the top number to find how many you need.

**76. Using a fraction upside down** — Multiplying by the bottom number and dividing by the top number.
*Hint:* Divide by the bottom number to share. Multiply by the top number for your total.

**77. Treating a percentage as money** — Subtracting the percentage digits directly as pounds or pence.
*Hint:* Percent means "out of 100". It is a proportion, not an exact amount of money!

**78. Percentage fraction confusion** — Confusing 25% with a half instead of a quarter.
*Hint:* 50% is a half. 25% is exactly half of that, which makes it one quarter!

**79. Dividing by a fraction error** — Adding the fraction or treating a half as one whole when dividing.
*Hint:* Think about it clearly. How many halves can you pour into one whole litre?

**80. Adding fractions straight across** — Adding the top numbers together and the bottom numbers together.
*Hint:* Make the bottom numbers match first! Once they match, only add the top numbers together.

**81. Forgetting to subtract the discount** — Calculating the discount correctly but stopping before taking it off the original price.
*Hint:* You found the discount, well done! Now subtract it from the starting price to finish.

**82. Fraction of the wrong whole** — Halving the starting total instead of the remaining amount in a multi-step fraction problem.
*Hint:* Read carefully! Are you finding a fraction of the total, or of what is left?

### Measurement

**83. Money place-value slip** — Dropping the pence and subtracting whole pounds only, or misaligning the pence.
*Hint:* Always line up the decimal points. Add place-holder zeros so the columns match perfectly.

**84. Adding instead of finding the difference** — Adding lengths or money together when asked for the change or amount left over.
*Hint:* Words like "change" or "left" usually mean subtraction. Take the amount away instead of adding!

**85. Miscount or rounding in measures** — Adding the item count to the price or multiplying by one fewer item.
*Hint:* Use multiplication for repeated groups. Take the exact price and multiply by the exact count.

**86. Incomplete money step** — Subtracting only the last item bought and ignoring the others, or leaving an item out of the total.
*Hint:* Find the cost of every single item together first. Then work out your change.

### Geometry

**87. Perimeter/area swap** — Working out the area when asked for perimeter, or vice versa.
*Hint:* Perimeter is the fence around the edge. Area is the space covering the grass inside.

**88. Incomplete perimeter** — Adding only two sides of a rectangle, or forgetting to double the sum of the length and width.
*Hint:* Walk all the way around the shape! A rectangle has four sides to add together.

**89. Wrong angle total** — Subtracting from 360° for a straight line or triangle, or confusing it with a right angle.
*Hint:* Angles on a straight line and inside a triangle always add up to exactly 180°.

**90. Composite-shape area slip** — Using the whole bounding box and forgetting the cut-out, or adding a cut-out instead of subtracting.
*Hint:* Find the area of the whole block first, then subtract the piece that was cut away.

**91. Coordinate read slip** — Reading the y-axis before the x-axis, or subtracting coordinates.
*Hint:* Always read along the corridor (x) before you read up the stairs (y).

### Statistics, Ratio & Proportion

**92. Unitary proportion slip** — Using the total price for one item, or multiplying without finding the single unit price first.
*Hint:* Find the price of just one single item first. Then multiply up to the new amount.

**93. Comparing prices ignoring quantity** — Subtracting or dividing pack prices without matching the number of items first.
*Hint:* It is not a fair fight yet! Work out the price for exactly one item first.

**94. Incomplete statistical total** — Leaving one bar or row out of a total, or comparing without combining first.
*Hint:* Double-check the chart. Have you added together every single row or bar you need?

**95. Reading the wrong statistical quantity** — Counting the number of categories instead of the data values.
*Hint:* Look at the axis labels. Are you counting the right things?

**96. Mean calculation slip** — Adding the scores but forgetting to divide, or dividing by the wrong amount.
*Hint:* The mean needs two steps. Add everything together, then divide by the number of items.

**97. Missing-value mean slip** — Averaging only the given numbers and ignoring the missing one.
*Hint:* Work backwards! Multiply the mean by the total number of items to find your grand total.
